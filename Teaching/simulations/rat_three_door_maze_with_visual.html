Why the answer is 9 minutes

First-step analysis

Let E be the expected remaining time at the start. Condition on the first door:

E=13(1)+13(3+E)+13(5+E)
E=3+23E⇒E=9minutes.
  1. Door 1: spend 1 minute and finish.
  2. Door 2: spend 3 minutes, return, and still expect E more minutes.
  3. Door 3: spend 5 minutes, return, and still expect E more minutes.
Why isn’t the answer (1 + 3 + 5) / 3 = 3?

That is the expected time spent on one attempt. With probability 2/3, that attempt leaves the rat back at the start. A complete run includes those repeated attempts. The rat makes 3 attempts on average: two returns averaging 4 minutes each, followed by a 1-minute exit.

Exact distribution and variability

For integer t, let f(t) = P(T = t). The histogram’s theoretical curve uses f(t) = [1{t = 1} + f(t − 3) + f(t − 5)] / 3, with f(t) = 0 for t ≤ 0.

The variance of escape time is 98 min². Individual runs can be long even though the mean is 9 minutes. At 20,000 independent trials, the theoretical standard error of the sample mean is √(98 / 20,000) = 0.07 minutes.

The displayed confidence interval is the sample mean ± 1.96 × (sample standard deviation / √n). It describes uncertainty in the mean, not a range containing 95% of individual escape times.

A path to discuss in class

Door 2 → Door 3 → Door 1 takes 3 + 5 + 1 = 9 minutes. Its probability is (1/3)³ = 1/27. Other paths also take 9 minutes, so P(T = 9) = 2/27.
